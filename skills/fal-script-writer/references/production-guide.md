# Script Writer

**Revision 2026-10-28a.** Name this revision in the delivery; an upload that
cannot quote it is an older copy of this skill.

A script for generated video is not a screenplay. Every shot is synthesised
independently by a model that has never seen the shot before it, so an unbroken
take, an "as previously", or a page of two-hander dialogue are all instructions
nothing downstream can execute. What ships is a **shot table**: discrete
generatable rows, a duration on each, a word-counted voiceover line, and an
explicit statement of what carries from the row above. Two modes, chosen once
and never mixed — **Cinematic** (acts, a turn, a resolution) and **Ad** (beats,
a clock, an offer) — and both feed `fal-video-production` when the ask is a whole
assembled ad, or `fal-video-generation` (one row, one clip),
`fal-audio-generation` (one clip per row, length-constrained) when it is not;
the continuity column stays in the table (a dedicated blocking skill is not
published).

## What makes this different from writing for camera

1. **Discrete shots, or nothing usable.** Write each row so it could be
   generated by someone who has read only that row; anything it needs from
   earlier — a face, a jacket, a kitchen, the time of day — is named again
   inside it. "Continues from before" generates a different scene.
2. **Timing is arithmetic, not feel.** Words per second is a number. Budget the
   script before writing a line of it (Step 1); hold every row to its cap.
3. **On-camera dialogue is the expensive way to say anything.** Lip-sync is the
   least reliable thing a video model does, and it spends a whole clip on a
   talking head. **Default to VO over visuals**; reserve on-camera speech for a
   line whose value is the performance, and give it its own row.

### Step 0b — Read the brief back before you write it

**A brief can be perfectly clear and still not fit.** Do this before writing a
line, and report it in the reply whether or not anything is wrong — a triage that
only speaks up on failure trains the reader to skip it.

Four questions, in this order:

1. **Does the beat list fit the shot budget?** Count the distinct visual beats the
   brief asks for — every named location change, every new object introduced, every
   escalation step. Compare that to the shot budget from Step 1 —
   `target ÷ 4s` at default pacing, more when a fast-paced brief buys
   short cuts. Report the ratio. **Over-subscription is the most common brief defect and it is
   invisible until assembly**: a 30-second piece naming twelve beats has seven
   shots to hold them, so five beats will be dropped by *someone*, and it should be
   the author rather than whoever renders it. Name which beats you would merge or
   cut, in priority order, and why.
2. **Is every structurally required moment actually a shot?** A piece that turns on
   a change — a transformation, a reveal, a before-and-after — needs the *causing
   moment* to be its own shot, and needs the shots either side to make the change
   legible. If the brief names the cause but the budget leaves no room to build to
   it, say so: the cause will read as arbitrary, and no amount of execution fixes a
   pivot that had nothing leading into it.
3. **Does anything the brief insists on have no room at all?** A character who must
   appear, a product that must be seen, a line that must be said. Each needs a
   shot or a window. If the budget is already full, that requirement is in conflict
   with the beat list, and the author chooses — not you, silently.
4. **What is genuinely underspecified?** Not "vague" — *unexecutable*. A brief can
   set a mood in one line and be entirely shootable. What matters is anything a row
   cannot be written without: what the thing looks like, where it is, what
   physically happens, and what carries from the row before.

**Then, if any answer is a conflict, ask once.** One card, header `Scope`,
declared in the roster at the end of this step. Put your recommended cut list in
the question text and offer `Use your cuts` / `Extend the runtime` / `I'll revise
the brief`. **With no answer, take your own cut list and proceed** — the beats you
dropped and the reason are then part of the delivery, not a silent loss. Never
stall a script on this; an over-subscribed brief still produces a shootable piece,
it just produces a shorter one than the author imagined.

**When you have to invent.** Count how many of these four the brief leaves
open: the product's benefit, the character's want, the offer, a claim. That
list is exhaustive — tone and audience are style choices the defaults table
already covers, not inventions. **Two or fewer:** invent, write, list them at
delivery as assumptions. **Three or more, or an invented benefit or offer:**
raise the `Assumptions` card — its text your drafted list in full, options
`Use these` / `Change them`. Unanswered, proceed with the drafted list and mark
it unconfirmed at delivery.

**Every card this skill can ever raise, declared here.** Ask with a card (on a host with no question tool, one plain message, then in
the same turn take the card's stated no-answer action and say so); never treat
an unanswered message as an answer. The first row is the intake
bundle above; the other four are conditional: each fires only when its
condition holds, and none of them blocks, because its row states the action
taken when no answer comes, so the pipeline never stalls on a card.

| Header | Fires when | Without an answer |
|---|---|---|
| header `Mode` / `Length` / `Voice` / `Aspect` (the intake bundle) | the brief leaves any of the five items open | fill from the defaults table |
| header `Scope` | Step 0b finds the beat list over-subscribed against the shot budget, or a required moment with no room | take the drafted cut list and proceed; the dropped beats and the reason go in the delivery |
| header `Assumptions` | three or more inventions, or an invented benefit or offer | proceed with the drafted list, marked unconfirmed at delivery |
| header `Claim substantiation` | supplied copy carries a regulated or superlative claim and the brief asserts no sign-off (Step 3) | the claim does not ship — Step 3 states exactly what is delivered instead |
| header `Must-say budget` | must-say lines alone exceed the target's word budget (guardrails) | deliver at the next length up, every line verbatim, override flagged first |

**Call arithmetic: at most two `ask_user_questions` calls per run.** The intake
bundle is the first; every conditional card that fires goes into **one** second
call, bundled. The cap counts calls, not questions.

If `Voice` is `No words`, write `—` in the VO and `W` columns; every word-count
check in Step 5 is then `n/a`, which is a pass.

## Step 1 — The word budget, before a word is written

Speaking rate, words per second: **2.3** measured/premium/sombre · **2.8**
conversational — **the default**, and roughly what TTS returns at default speed
· **3.3** urgent retail or fast comedy, above which you cut copy instead. Apply
a **0.9 headroom factor** so lines land inside their shot rather than butting
the cut, making every budget here `floor(seconds x rate x 0.9)` — applied to the
whole script and to each shot's `Cut`. At the 2.8 default: **30s carries 75
words, total.** 15s carries 37. 60s carries 151. An 8-second shot carries 20. A
2-second hook carries **five**.

Compute it in one sandbox pass, never by eye — one mechanism, so the numbers in
the delivered table are the numbers you checked:

```python
rate, cuts = 2.8, [2.0, 4.0, 10.0]          # cut seconds, one per shot
print([int(s * rate * 0.9) for s in cuts], sum(cuts), int(sum(cuts) * rate * 0.9))
```

### Cut length is not generated length

A model generates in the lengths its endpoint allows, not the length your beat
wants. Verified against the live schemas: **H3 Max, the default route, takes a
number of seconds from `0.92` to `15`** (decimals allowed; the output can run
about 0.7s longer than asked), so a 2-second hook is generated at 2 seconds.
Most fallbacks floor higher (Wan 3.0 Prime takes integers from `2`): **Seedance 2.5, the cross-family fallback, takes
whole seconds `4` to `30`** (bare integers as strings — `"4"`, not `"4s"`;
Seedance 2.0 stops at `15`); **Veo 3.1 Fast takes only `4s`,
`6s` or `8s`** and defaults to `8s`; **Kling v3 Pro takes `3` to `15`**, default
`5`; base MiniMax H3 takes integers `5` to `15`. On those routes a 2-second hook
is generated at the route's shortest allowed length and trimmed in the edit.

**Pacing sets your shot budget; the route floor prices it.** At default pacing
`Cut ≈ Gen` at about 4 seconds a shot, so a 30-second piece holds about **7**
shots. A fast-paced brief — TikTok, frenetic, montage — is allowed to break that
ceiling the way the hook already does: every row cut at 1–2.5 seconds. On H3 Max
`Gen` can then match `Cut`; on a fallback route each row is generated at the
route's minimum, so eight quick shots in a 15-second ad bill about 32 generated
seconds on Seedance or Veo. Either way the budget is set by cost rather than
arithmetic, so report the generated total in the delivery and let the shot count
follow the brief's energy. A beat list that exceeds even that is cut
by the author in Step 0b, never silently at assembly.

**Default to the route `fal-video-generation` actually uses, which is H3 Max**
— do not re-derive a route from this paragraph. Rows with photoreal people still plan on H3 Max;
a shot H3 Max refuses moves to Veo 3.1 Fast (`4s`/`6s`/`8s`) per
`fal-video-generation`'s moderation rule, so say in the delivery that such a
row's `Gen` may rise to the next Veo length (4s, 6s or 8s; 8s for a reference
row) at Veo 3.1 Fast's rate, so quote that cost too; a row that fails `fal-video-generation`'s Veo fit test goes back to the user if refused. This skill picking a different one
silently overrides the video skill's whole routing table: a run whose rows were
planned against Veo's three-value enum came back `4/4/8/8/6/4` for a
`2/4/8/8/5/3` contract and had to be rescued by trimming, purely because this step
named Veo first. Choose otherwise only for a stated reason — a row cut longer than
8s excludes Veo; a row that must render under 4s rules out Seedance and Veo, under 5s also rules out base H3, and under 6s rules out LTX 2.5 — and say which route you
took and why, so the video skill can honour it rather than re-decide.

Every row therefore carries two numbers: `Cut`, what the finished video uses and
what must sum to target, and `Gen`, what to ask the video skill for, with
`Gen >= Cut` always and `Gen` a length the route actually offers.

**One rule for choosing `Gen`, not three.** Take the shortest allowed length that
is at least `Cut`, on the route the video skill will use. Confirm the route's
allowed lengths from its schema rather than from this paragraph — these change,
and an earlier draft of this skill asserted Veo was fixed at 8 seconds, which the
schema disproves. If the route is genuinely undecided, pick the candidate whose
allowed lengths cover your **longest** `Cut` — Veo 3.1 Fast caps at 8s, so any
row cut longer than that excludes it — then take every row's `Gen` from that
one route's schema, and say which routes you checked.

## Step 2A — Cinematic mode

Write four one-sentence lines first — the reason the shots are in that order,
and part of the delivery.

- **Premise** — who, where, and what is about to be disturbed.
- **Want** — what the character is chasing, visible in action.
- **Need** — what they actually lack. Want and need must not be the same thing.
- **Turn** — the single moment the want stops working. Name the shot number.

Then shape the rows: **setup** (the world, plus the want made physical) →
**pressure** (the want fails once) → **the turn** → **escalation** (a costlier
attempt) → **resolution** (the need answered, or refused). At 60s that is
roughly 8s / 16s / 8s / 16s / 12s; scale proportionally. Under 30 seconds drop
escalation, not the turn — a piece with no turn is a mood reel. Cast **two
speaking characters at most**, each with a one-word ALL-CAPS tag reused verbatim
in every row, so staging can be read off the table as-is.

| Symptom in the draft | What is actually wrong | Structural fix |
|---|---|---|
| Every row is the same temperature | no turn; the want never fails | show the want *working* in row 1, then break it at the midpoint |
| The last row explains itself in VO | the need was never dramatised | give the resolution a physical action that only reads if the need was met — then delete the line |
| The middle rows are interchangeable | repetition posing as escalation | each attempt costs more than the last; raise the price, not the volume |
| Two characters talk for three rows | dialogue is carrying the plot | convert to VO over visuals, keep at most one on-camera line |
| Nothing can be cut to reach target | two premises in one piece | delete the second premise, never the turn |

## Step 2B — Ad mode

Six beats, budgeted at 30s and rate 2.8. At any other length: drop the beats
the `Cut it when` column names, scale each surviving `Cut` by `target / 30` in
whole seconds — trimming or padding **Demo** so the column still sums exactly —
then recompute every `Words` cap with the Step 1 formula. When supplied
verbatim copy fixes a row's word count instead, invert that arithmetic and
derive the duration from the words: `Cut = ceil(W / (rate x 0.9))`.

| Beat | Job | 30s cut | Words | Cut it when | Fold into |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Stop the scroll. One image, one idea. | 2s | 5 | never | — |
| Problem | The cost of not having it, shown not stated. | 4s | 10 | length ≤ 15s | the hook, as the "before" half |
| Demo | The product doing the thing. The only mandatory beat. | 10s | 25 | never | — |
| Proof | Why believe it — but see Step 3. | 6s | 15 | no substantiated claim exists | drop entirely, extend Demo |
| Offer | What to do, where, by when. | 5s | 12 | brand film with no CTA | the tagline card |
| Tagline | Logo, line, hold. | 3s | 7 | never | — |

Durations sum to 30s; words sum to 74, inside the 75-word budget.

**Beats are not shots.** At fast pacing — TikTok, frenetic — a beat, usually
Demo, splits into several rows of 1–2.5 second cuts (a montage), each generated
at its `Cut` on H3 Max (at the route floor and trimmed on a fallback), each row still one action with its anchors
restated. The beat's words divide across its rows and the caps apply per row.

**When verbatim copy consumes the clock at full length** — nothing in `Cut it
when` fires, yet must-say words leave no room for six beats — verbatim copy
outranks beat count. Fold beats using their own `Fold into` cells, in the order
Problem, then Proof, then Offer, until the remaining rows fit, and name every
fold in the delivery.

**The hook is not an intro.** Five words and one legible image; a logo sting in
the first two seconds spends the hook on a beat the tagline already owns.

## Step 3 — Continuity, and claims

**The `Carries in` column is the only continuity that exists.** Write it as
named, describable properties — `WARDROBE`, `LOCATION`, `PROP`, `TIME`,
`PALETTE`, character tag — each restated inside that row's shot seed. Two tags
may depend on something stronger, and both are planned, never assumed:

- **`FRAME`** — starts on the previous row's last frame. Downstream extracts
  that frame and passes it as the start image to an image-to-video route. Cap
  at one `FRAME` link per script unless the user asks for more.
- **`MAP`** — two or more characters whose positions must not swap. Record per
  row each character's screen side, facing and eyeline in `Carries in`, and
  keep them identical across rows that cut together.

Anything else reading as "same as the last shot" is a defect; Step 5 greps for it.

**Claims.** An ad script puts words in a brand's mouth. **Never invent** a
statistic, a percentage, a superlative (`best`, `#1`, `fastest`), a comparative
(`twice as long as`), an award, a rating, a review or an endorsement — not as a
placeholder, not as an example, not in on-screen text where it reads as fine
print. If Proof has nothing substantiated to say, cut it per the beat table.

**A claim the user typed is not a claim the user can make.** Where supplied copy
touches health, safety, medical, financial or environmental territory, or any
superlative or comparative, it needs substantiation — and you make that call by
reading the copy itself, never by reading an intake answer. Two sources satisfy
it:

- **The brief asserts a sign-off** — "legal signed off on this exact wording",
  "we hold the study". That counts as answered; record the assertion as the
  source in check 8.
- **The `Claim substantiation` card** (header declared in Step 0), listing each
  such line verbatim, options `We hold evidence for all of these` / `Remove
  them` / `I'll revise them`.

An unsubstantiated claim does not ship — even verbatim, even must-say;
substantiation outranks the must-say guardrail. With neither source, deliver
the script with that line omitted, mark its row, list the omission in the
delivery note, and raise the card so an answer can reinstate the line in a
revision. Never stall the run waiting on the card.

## Step 4 — Write the rows

1. Fill `Cut` for every row and make the column sum to target **before** writing
   any copy — copy expands to fill the space it is given. Then compute each
   row's `W` cap with the Step 1 script.
2. Write the VO line and count it. Over cap, cut words — never raise the rate,
   never lengthen the shot to fit the line.
3. Write the shot seed as a self-contained visual: subject, wardrobe, location,
   light, framing, camera move. Present tense, one action, and everything the
   `Carries in` cell names restated in full. Find `fal-shot-craft` with
   `search_guidance` and load it with `read_guidance` (`kind: "skill"`, with the returned `id` and `version_id`) for how to word it — slot-composed framing, a motivated or
   explicitly locked camera, the specific gesture rather than the named
   emotion — and print its validator lines per row. If it will not load,
   this item's minimums stand and the delivery says the craft reference was
   unavailable.
4. Fill `On-screen text` (ad) with the literal string to burn, or `—`; under six
   words, echoing the VO rather than duplicating it. Then fill `Gen`.

## Step 5 — Verification: arithmetic, not taste

Run all of these and answer each explicitly. Checks 6 to 9 are conditional; an
inapplicable check is `n/a`, which is a pass.

1. **Every row has a numeric `Cut`.** No blanks, no ranges, no "approx".
2. **`Cut` sums to the target**, exactly, and report the sum. A 31s cut of a
   30s spot is a fail, not a rounding.
3. **Every row has `Gen >= Cut`**, and `Gen` is a length the route can produce.
4. **Per-row words `<= floor(Cut x rate x 0.9)`.** List every row and its cap;
   one overrun fails the check.
5. **Total VO words `<= floor(target x rate x 0.9)`.** Report both numbers.
6. *(any row depending on an earlier one)* **No `Carries in` cell says
   "continues", "same as above", "as before", or names the previous shot's final
   frame** — unless tagged `FRAME` and planned in Step 3. Grep the `Carries in`
   column only; a whole-document grep flags the sentence reporting this check.
7. *(ad only)* **Hook `Cut` <= 2.0s and hook words <= 5**, and no logo or brand
   name occupies row 1's visual. The restriction is the image only — the VO may
   name the brand, since a must-say line is allowed to open the film.
8. *(any claim present)* **Every number, superlative and comparative traces to a
   user-supplied line Step 3 records as substantiated** — a sign-off asserted in
   the brief, or an answered `Claim substantiation` card. List them with their
   sources; a size, volume or count printed on the product counts as substantiated, with the label as its source; a printed performance, health or safety figure ("SPF 50", "24 h cold") still needs substantiation. A line Step 3 withheld appears nowhere, including
   on-screen text.
9. *(on-camera dialogue only)* **No row has two speaking characters**, and every
   on-camera line sits in a row of at least 3s. Count the rows with dialogue: if
   it is most of them, the piece should have been VO.

Fix and re-run. If a script fails check 2 or 4 twice, the target length is wrong
for the piece. Do not compress a third time: write the script at the next length
up (15s → 30s → 60s → 90s), deliver it with the override flagged first in the
delivery, and list what staying at the brief's length would have required
cutting. This escalation needs no answer from anyone; when the cause is must-say
overflow, the `Must-say budget` card is optional and additive on top of it.

## Step 6 — Deliver

Hand back, in order: mode and target; the four cinematic lines or the beat
budget; the filled table; the Step 5 numbers; then the hand-off. Name every
field filled from the defaults table, and everything invented.

**If the brief is a whole finished ad, hand off once, not three times.** A request
that wants clips *and* narration *and* music *and* on-screen text assembled into a
delivered file goes to **`fal-video-production`**, with this table as its stage-1
output. Hand it the filled table and the Step 1 word budgets intact — it derives
each shot's audio placement from the `dur` column, so the windows computed here are
its contract, not a suggestion. Splitting the same brief across the three hand-offs
below instead is how an ad ends up with clips nobody reviewed, narration bunched
into the opening seconds and on-screen text that never got burned: each sibling
does its own part correctly and no one owns the joins.

If `fal-video-production` will not load, take the hand-offs below and **say
plainly that nothing is then orchestrating them** — the caller has to sequence the
stages and burn any on-screen text last, after every re-render.

For a single shot, a single clip, or a script the user will assemble themselves,
the individual hand-offs are correct:

- → **`fal-video-generation`** — one row = one clip: shot seed, `Gen`, aspect.
- → **`fal-audio-generation`** — the VO column **one clip per row**, each with that
  row's target window, so narration is generated to length rather than trimmed.
  Never hand it the column as a single continuous script: a one-piece read has to
  be cut apart afterwards by detecting silence, and it fails by crowding every
  word into the first seconds and leaving the tail silent.
- **`MAP` and `FRAME` rows** travel with the clip hand-off: `MAP` rows keep
  their screen side, facing and eyeline in `Carries in`; for `FRAME`, extract
  the previous clip's last frame and pass it as the start image to an
  image-to-video route.

**House rules that do not apply here:** 2, 3, 5 and 11 govern endpoint schemas,
optimise flags and sandbox uploads; 4 governs edit models redrawing assets; 15
and 19 measure change in a generated artifact. This skill submits nothing, names
no parameters and generates nothing, so all of those land on the downstream
skills — including the named-fallback rule: with no model calls there is no
primary route to fall back from, so no fallback is named. 1, 6–10, 12, 13, 14
(the hand-offs above are pipeline hand-offs, not routing), 16, 17 and 18 are
applied above.

## Guardrails

- **A row a model cannot generate alone is not a row.** If you cannot delete
  every other row and still generate this one, it is under-specified.
- **Two speaking characters, maximum**, and never two in one row — crosstalk and
  overlapping dialogue do not survive generation.
- **Never put text in a shot seed.** On-screen text lives in its own column and
  is burned in the edit; models render lettering unreliably, and a misspelled
  brand name in frame is a reshoot.
- **Must-say lines are load-bearing.** They go in verbatim and count against the
  budget like everything else — never paraphrase one, never raise the rate to
  fit one, whatever the brief instructs. If they alone exceed the target's
  budget, the arithmetic decides on its own: report the numbers (their word
  count, the budget, the seconds they need at the stated rate), then take the
  Step 5 escalation — the next length up, every line intact, override flagged
  first in the delivery. Optionally raise the `Must-say budget` card (declared
  in Step 0; one option per line to cut, plus `Go to the longer length`), but
  never wait on it — unanswered, the longer script stands.
- **Not the clean case.** A brief that is already a finished script → convert it
  to rows and run Step 5 on it, reporting failures rather than rewriting the
  voice. A mood with no product or character → invent nothing, go to the
  `Assumptions` card. Under 8 seconds → hook and tagline only, and say the other
  beats do not fit. More than about a dozen rows for 30 seconds at default
  pacing, or a generated total past roughly three times the finished length at
  fast pacing → the cutting is faster than generated clips hold up under;
  consolidate and say why.
